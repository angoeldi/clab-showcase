# References for the MI Social Media Domain Config

This file documents the academic and professional sources that informed the **domain yaml** and the concrete design choices (stages, intervention style, planning options, and caveats about the evidence).

## Motivational Interviewing core theory and mechanisms

Design choices in `domain_mi_problematic_social_media_use_2026-02-07.yaml`:

- **Stage model (Engage, Focus, Evoke, Plan)** and MI definition as a collaborative, person-centered method of guiding to elicit motivation.
- **MI spirit** (partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation) and emphasis on autonomy.
- **OARS** micro-skills and reflective listening as the default interaction mode.
- **Elicit-Provide-Elicit** (ask permission before advice; provide neutrally; elicit the user's response).
- **Change talk / commitment language** as a relevant process variable (hence the explicit "evoke change talk" prompts).

## Evidence base for MI as a behavior-change method

Design choice:

- The agent is framed as a **behavior-change** coach (not advice-giving), because MI has evidence across diverse behaviors and health contexts, and tends to outperform "traditional advice giving" in many applications.

## Problematic social media use and behavioral addiction framing

Design choices:

- Use the term **problematic social media use** unless the user self-identifies with "addiction".
- Treat behavioral-addiction-like symptom language as a *descriptive lens* (salience, tolerance, withdrawal, conflict, relapse), not a diagnosis.
- Keep **risk** broad (functional impairment, distress, safety) because high distress or comorbidities may warrant clinical support.

## Planning options: behavior change techniques and self-regulation

Design choices:

- Planning uses **small experiments**, **self-monitoring**, **stimulus control / friction**, and **implementation intentions** (if-then plans).
- These are offered as a **menu** (autonomy-supportive), not as prescriptions.

## Empirical evidence on reducing social media use: mixed, so avoid over-promising

Design choices:

- The agent avoids claiming that abstinence or strict limits *will* improve well-being; instead it frames changes as **experiments** and encourages review.
- The tutorial explicitly warns against perfectionism and supports iterative adaptation.

Sources (illustrative, not exhaustive):

- Hunt, M. G., Marx, R., Lipson, C., & Young, J. (2018). No More FOMO: Limiting Social Media Decreases Loneliness and Depression. *Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology*, 37(10), 751-768. https://doi.org/10.1521/jscp.2018.37.10.751
